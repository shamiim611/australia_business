RESEARCH SERIES / PART 01

Connected, but
unevenly capable.

How have digital capabilities spread across Australian businesses—and who is being left behind?

The supplied surveys span 2005–06 to 2024–25. Internet connectivity is now almost universal. The more revealing questions concern what businesses do with it, and how participation differs by size and industry.

02 / BEYOND ACCESS

Connectivity is only one dimension.

Cloud, operational systems, data analysis and digital management describe different activities. Their individual adoption rates tell us where use is widespread; they cannot tell us how many capabilities the same business holds.

Fixed 0–100% scale. Named activities remain separate; percentages need not add to 100. † Caution; !! too unreliable for a chart; — unavailable.

03 / THE SIZE DIVIDE

More adoption does not always mean a smaller gap.

Gap = the 200+ employee rate minus the 0–4 employee rate, in percentage points. The 0–4 band is the study's micro-business comparison group; it is not the whole small-business sector. Results are unadjusted for industry composition and are not significance tests.

04 / SECTOR DIFFERENCES

The national average hides different patterns of use.

Compare each industry with the national benchmark in the two approved recent waves. Being below average can reflect business models as well as constraints; it is not automatically evidence of poor performance.

Cells are percentage points relative to the national rate. Blue = below; green = above. Counts cover exactly two waves, not long-run persistence. † flags a cautious input. Unflagged waves provide a sensitivity check.

05 / WHAT HOLDS BUSINESSES BACK?

Skills, finance and uncertainty coexist.

C: separate survey snapshots. These ICT-specific constraint profiles do not establish that infrastructure and cost barriers have been replaced by skills barriers. All businesses are the base; multiple responses are allowed.

06 / CAPABILITY AND INNOVATION

Innovation activity is associated with digital participation.

In 2018–19, innovation-active businesses reported higher online-order receipt within each published employment-size band. The differences are descriptive: innovation may influence digital adoption, digital adoption may support innovation, and other factors may influence both.

2018–19 only. Each innovation-status population is its own denominator. These tables condition separately on size or industry, never both together. Innovation definitions must be rechecked before extending across years.

What this study can—and cannot—establish
